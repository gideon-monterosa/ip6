= Constructs

== Outcome Constructs

1. Productivity

Definition
Perceived ability to make meaningful progress on work tasks within a meeting or during a workday.

Levels
- Meeting-level: Contribution of a meeting to work progress (ROTI / usefulness)
- Daily-level: Overall productivity rating at end of day
- Survey-level: General perception about how meetings affect productivity

2. Flow (Deep Focus)

Definition
Ability to engage in sustained, uninterrupted, cognitively demanding work.

Levels
- Meeting-level: Focus disruption rating
- Daily-level: Amount of deep work done
- Survey-level: Perceived flow disruption due to meetings

3. Well-Being

Definition
Immediate affective and energetic state influenced by meetings.

Dimensions
- Energy (drained ↔ energized)
- Emotional valence (negative ↔ positive)
- Survey-level job satisfaction / stress perception

== Characteristic Constructs

4. Meeting Load

Definition
Total exposure to meetings within a day.

Metrics
- Daily meeting count
- Total meeting duration
- % of workday in meetings

5. Workday Fragmentation

Definition
The extent to which meetings interrupt or break up potential deep work periods, reducing the length and continuity of focus blocks.

Metrics
- Number of Focus Blocks ≥ X Minutes
- Fragmentation Index/Score

6. Meeting Timing

Definition
Time-of-day placement of meetings.

Categories
- Morning
- Midday
- Afternoon

7. Meeting Usefulness (ROTI)

Definition
Perceived return on time invested in a meeting.

Metrics
ROTI (Return on time invested): “This meeting was worth my time.”

== Perception & Alignment Constructs

8. Perceived Meeting Impact (Survey-Level Construct)

Definition
Participants’ global beliefs about how meetings affect them.

Examples:

- “Meetings disrupt my flow.”
- “On high-meeting days I’m less productive.”
- “Morning meetings are most disruptive.”

9. Real-Time Experienced Impact (Field-Level Construct)

Definition
Momentary and daily lived experience measured during the field study.

Includes:
- Meeting feedbacks
- End of Day feedbacks
- Objective calendar metrics

10. Perception–Reality Alignment

Definition
Degree to which survey-reported beliefs correspond to observed real-time effects.

Examples:
Person says: “Morning meetings are worst.”
Field data shows: Morning meetings have highest disruption.

= Feedback Surveys

== Meeting Feedback Survey

Q1 Did this meeting disrupt your focus?

Likert Scale:
1 = Not at all
5 = Very much

Q2 How do you feel after this meeting?

Categories:
- Negative
- Neutral
- Positive

Q3 How energized do you feel after this meeting?

Likert Scale:
1 = drained
5 = energized

Q4 This meeting was worth my time.

Likert Scale:
1 = strongly disagree
5 = strongly agree

Q5 What were the main issues? / positive

Connected to the ROTI Score show negative reasons for < 2 and positive for > 3

Multiple Choice:

Negative:

- No clear agenda
- Unclear purpose
- No clear outcome or decision
- Meeting could have been an email
- Too long
- Too many participants
- Interrupted an important focus block
- Too close to another meeting
- Scheduled at a bad time
- No relevance to my work
- Mentally draining
- Frustrating discussion

Positive:

- Clear agenda
- Clear purpose
- Clear decisions made
- Efficient and well structured
- Helped me make progress
- Provided important information
- Clarified next steps
- Scheduled at a good time
- Energizing discussion
- Motivating
- Strengthened team alignment
